Number two. A concept-map rubric that may flag struggling students early. In this Academic Medicine innovation report, a Harvard team built a five-domain rubric for scoring student-made "mechanism maps" from 134 first-year students. The domains are comprehensiveness, analytic detail, organization, accuracy, and overall quality. Four raters agreed well in most domains, with accuracy the weakest. For one case, each one-point higher score roughly doubled the odds of finishing above the bottom quartile on the exam, even after adjusting for quiz scores. Why it matters: an ungraded reasoning exercise could double as an early signal. The authors call for validation at other schools first.

Number three. Faculty burnout: 13 percent at some schools, 41 percent at others. This JAMA Network Open study used the AAMC StandPoint survey of more than 15,000 faculty at 28 US medical schools between 2021 and 2024. Pooled burnout was 32 percent. Commitment ranged from 74 to 95 percent across schools, and satisfaction from 62 to 86 percent. Only 67 of 502 departments, about 13 percent, had both low burnout and high commitment. Autonomy, purpose, and belonging helped explain burnout at the department level. The synthesis: track burnout and engagement together, and go find the bright-spot departments to learn from.
