☕ Coffee break with Beth
In my own words. Written by Beth, not AI.

The food insecurity study from Yale is somewhat staggering. Even with a low-ish survey response rate (28%), finding over 30% of trainees to have food insecurity is wild. They had 17 peds trainees (25% met criteria for food insecurity). They say that there may have been selection bias and also location cost of living impacts — but an earlier multi site study with higher participation rate still found 14% amongst trainees. Seems like meals at educational conferences might be more than a nice-to-have incentive for many …

- Beth

Almost 1 in 3 trainees screened food insecure, and interns had it worst

  • Anonymous survey at Yale New Haven: 164 residents and fellows (about 28% of ~600 eligible) completed the 10-item USDA module.
  • 30.5% were food insecure; PGY-1s 45.3% vs 23.4% of later years (OR 2.71).
  • Lack of money was strongly tied to food insecurity (75% vs 4%); lack of time was not.
Try this: add a validated food-security screen to your intern check-ins, and treat conference meals and move-in support as real resources, not perks.
Mahan E, Demiray A, Huot SJ, Criscione J, Vazquez M. JAMA Netw Open. 2026;9(10):e2640774. Published online Oct 26, 2026. doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2026.40774

TeachingUME

A 90-minute microteaching workshop builds confident near-peer teachers

  • IM clerkship students gave a 5-minute talk with feedback in week 1, then a revised talk in the final week.
  • Vs peers at other Harvard sites (41/51 vs 21/103 responded), confidence teaching rose more (beta 0.72) and so did confidence giving peer feedback (beta 0.77).
  • Now part of the IM clerkship and residency program; can run virtually.
Try this: borrow the format for intern orientation: one short talk, peer feedback, and a redo at the end of the block.
Zhou JC, Krupat E, Gottlieb BR, Kumar NL, Petersen EM, Osman NY. Acad Med. Online Oct 29, 2026. doi:10.1093/acamed/wvag343

We barely teach how to say "I'm not sure": a scoping review

  • 18,810 records screened; only 21 studies made the cut, mostly UME/GME and mostly about diagnostic uncertainty.
  • Methods were experiential (simulation, SPs, OSCEs, role-play); fewer than half involved patients.
  • Mean MERSQI 10.4, and no study measured clinical practice or patient outcomes.
Synthesis: a wide-open scholarship gap. Add an uncertainty-talk station to your next family-centered rounds sim and measure it.
Papanagnou D, Dahm MR, Adamczyk A, et al., Santhosh L. Acad Med (scoping review). Online Oct 28, 2026. doi:10.1093/acamed/wvag342
