☕ Coffee break with Beth
In my own words. Written by Beth, not AI.

I like the Job Demands vs Resources article pending publication in Academic Medicine. On the one hand, the demand vs resource idea from the business world seems very simple. And yet it is also a powerful model for shifting focus off of trainees as a source of blame when it comes to their own burnout, and back onto our systems. I think this has particular relevance as lots of generational differences have led to a more alluring temptation to blame trainees for being burned out… simultaneous with increasingly strained systems that are not supplying adequate resources to get our day to day work done.

- Beth

Honors gaps persist across 5 schools, but structure can shrink them

  • 17,513 clerkship grades from 3,012 third-year students at 5 US schools (2016-2023). Gender, race/ethnicity, and SES gaps in Honors persisted after adjusting for medical knowledge.
  • Overall Honors rates were similar in LICs vs block rotations (40% vs 42%), but URM students were more likely to get Honors in LICs (aOR 1.73), as were non-URM minority students (aOR 1.77).
  • Grading committees didn't change overall Honors rates, but narrowed the gap in which men got Honors less often than women (aOR 1.29).
Why it matters: clerkship grades feed straight into our residency applications. A grading committee is a fairly cheap fix to try.
Lockspeiser T, Christensen W, Colson E, Aagaard E, Artino AR Jr, et al., Chen HC. Acad Med (Research Report). Online Oct 24, 2026. doi:10.1093/acamed/wvag340

Catch it early: monitoring plans go with fewer formal learning plans

  • 470 residents in 42 programs at one Canadian institution (2019-2025); plans were coded as monitoring, minor, or major.
  • Monitoring plans were linked to a lower chance of later minor (beta -0.26) and major (beta -0.17) plans. Minor plans did not predict major ones.
  • Four program profiles emerged, from monitoring-heavy to intensive-plan-heavy, pointing to local culture.
Try this: make a written, low-stakes "monitoring" plan a normal CCC outcome, not a scarlet letter. Remember it's an association, not proof of cause.
Hamza DM. Acad Med (Research Report). Online Oct 20, 2026. doi:10.1093/acamed/wvag338

A roadmap for AI in each of the 5 parts of CBME

  • The ICBME Collaborative (Lomis, Pusic, Warm, Hauer, and others) maps AI onto all 5 CBME core components using socio-technical systems theory.
  • Examples: LLMs tagging narrative comments to Milestones/EPAs, nudges when a resident has enough evidence for more autonomy, and cohort summaries that show who gets less feedback.
  • They call for AI literacy, learner involvement, and rigorous evaluation for fairness.
Synthesis: a great menu for a CCC or faculty retreat: pick one component and one tool, then measure it.
Lomis KD, Pusic MV, Warm E, et al., Hauer KE. Perspect Med Educ (Eye Opener, open access). Published Oct 22, 2026. doi:10.5334/pme.2594
