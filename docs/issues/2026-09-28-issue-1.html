"Readiness for what?" How pediatric PDs decide a graduate is practice-ready

  • In a grounded theory study, 15 pediatric PDs described an "immense moral responsibility" for the practice-readiness call.
  • Definitions split three ways: ready as a general pediatrician regardless of career plans, ready for the specific setting the resident is headed to, or ready for fellowship.
Why it matters: the ABP moves to EPA-based readiness attestations in 2028. Programs and CCCs need a shared answer to "ready for what?" before then.
"Program Directors' Varied Conceptions of Practice Readiness." Pediatrics. Online Sept 2, 2026. PMID 42680169. doi:10.1542/peds.2026-077198

2Peds GMESelection

Screening season: what actually predicts pediatric intern performance?

  • ERAS metrics (518 residents, 10 programs): GHHS (B=0.15, P=.009) and Step 2 CK (B=0.06, P=.007) were associated with overall Milestone performance.
  • Medical school metrics (414 graduates, 5 sites; Milestones for 228): the NBME Pediatrics shelf was the only factor tied to all 6 competencies, and peds clerkship honors predicted Professionalism and Patient Care.
Synthesis: effects are small, and Milestone data were missing for about 45% of graduates in the second study. Use these as nudges in holistic review, not cutoffs.
J Grad Med Educ. Aug 2026. doi:10.4300/jgme-d-25-00854.1 and doi:10.4300/jgme-d-25-01139.1

The "unknowable" intern: what PDs wish they'd known at handover

  • 19 PDs across FM, IM, OB, peds, and surgery shared 38 stories of residents who struggled. The surprises were usually workplace adaptation, not knowledge.
  • Three kinds of information gaps: known but ambiguous, knowable but absent, and currently unknowable until the resident arrives.
Try this: add adaptation signals (asking for help, workflow, team integration) to your early-intern check-ins.
Bowen JL, Cooper DM, Larson RW, et al. Acad Med. 2026;101(10):1289-96. doi:10.1093/acamed/wvag120
