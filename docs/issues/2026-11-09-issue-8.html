Your 2025 med ed reading list, curated by humans: 11 papers out of 2,996

Is AI cheating? Most clerkship directors aren't sure what the rules are

  • National survey of IM clerkship directors (Sep-Dec 2024): 114/141 schools responded (80.9%).
  • 39.5% reported no formal AI policy and 34.2% didn't know if one existed. AI on a reflection essay was "cheating" to 42.9% vs 5.2% for clinical diagnosis.
  • 73.7% would encourage AI for at least one clinical skill, but only 6.1% said their institution offers firewall-protected AI tools.
Why it matters: residents supervise these students. Agree on a unit-level AI norm (what's OK, what needs disclosure) before students guess.
McQuade CN, Kumar NL, Schaye V, Olson APJ, Hufmeyer KK, McBee E, Kisielewski M, Osman NY, Bonifacino E. Acad Med. Online Nov 5, 2026. doi:10.1093/acamed/wvag350

RemediationUME

Remediation once, risk again: what 758 medical students teach us

  • Five cohorts (matriculated 2014-2018): 146/758 (19.3%) needed at least one pre-clinical block remediation; 81 remediated 2-5 blocks.
  • No exam-trend pattern flagged who would struggle, but prior remediation raised the odds of future block struggles.
  • Any remediation was tied to extra Step 1 study time and more first-attempt Step 1 failures; the number of remediations was not.
Synthesis: a struggle is a signal, not a sentence. Pair academic plans with social and well-being support, and most learners go on to thrive.
Anstey JE, Haedinger LA, Philipp TC, Schneider BN, Albright A, Carney PA, Pope BA. Med Teach. Online Nov 3, 2026. doi:10.1080/0142159X.2026.2749548
