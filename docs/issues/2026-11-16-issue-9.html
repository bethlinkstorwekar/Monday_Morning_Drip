That same theme runs through this week: clinical teachers who feel judged by student evaluations that miss their world, a call for a shared vocabulary so coaching can be studied, and new reviews on belonging and on what it means when students feel unmotivated.

Interview season check: applicants are choosing a learning environment, not a feature list

  • Scoping review of 65 studies (2015-June 2026, US/Canada) in 41 journals; 95.4% were surveys, mostly author-built.
  • 1,237 selection factors (about 19 per study), mapped to a Learning Environment framework: social 25.6%, organizational 25.3%, personal 24.7%, societal 20.6%.
  • Physical and virtual space factors were rarely studied (2.4%).
Try this: audit your interview day by domain. Do applicants see the social and organizational culture (residents, feedback, support), or just the rotation schedule?

FacultyAssessment

"This is just not their world": clinical teachers on student evaluations of teaching

  • Toronto Department of Medicine interviews: 10 faculty leaders and 15 frontline clinical teachers across subspecialties.
  • Clinical workload and relationships with learners shaped how faculty experienced SETs; many felt departments didn't respond to their concerns, leaving them isolated and undervalued.
  • Many doubted SETs were fair or useful for high-stakes decisions without safeguards. Suggested alternatives: peer review, third-party observation, and credit for mentoring and curriculum work.
Why it matters: if learner evaluations feed promotion files, pair them with direct observation or peer review and tell faculty how the data are used.
Lee C, Chandran N, Dhamko H, Bui E, McDonald-Blumer H, Brydges R. Med Teach. Online Nov 11, 2026. doi:10.1080/0142159X.2026.2756415

Coach, mentor, advisor, therapist? A shared dictionary for coaching programs

  • A Scholarly Perspective from the CHARM Coaching Network proposes standard definitions across 5 dimensions: coaching format, modality, type, framework, and focus.
  • Separates professional (developmental) coaching from mentoring, advising, and therapy that use a "coach approach."
  • Describes the core coaching process and the difference between coach training, certification, credentialing, and accreditation.
Try this: before you launch or evaluate a resident coaching program, write down which kind of coaching it is, using these terms, so your outcomes can be compared.
